The Plant Kingdom & Golden Fractals (Ch. 6 & Ch. 7)¶

Source Locus: Chapter 6 (pp. 247–268) & Chapter 7 (pp. 269–292 · Proofs pp. 331–332, by Ana Lúcia Braz Dias)

  1. Botanical Phyllotaxis & The Golden Angle (Ch. 6): Successive primordia in sunflower capitula, pinecones, and spiral phyllotaxis diverge by the Golden Angle:

    \[\theta_\phi = \frac{360^\circ}{\phi^2} = 360^\circ(2 - \phi) \approx 137.50776^\circ \quad \left(\text{complement } \frac{360^\circ}{\phi} \approx 222.49224^\circ\right).\]

    Because \(\phi\) has the slowest-converging continued fraction [1; 1, 1, ...], this divergence minimizes radial overlap and produces visible parastichy spiral counts in consecutive Fibonacci pairs \((F_k, F_{k+1})\) (cross-linked to geometor/phyllotaxis).

  2. POS-7.01 · Symmetric Binary Golden Fractal Tree (:math:`r = 1/phi`): A symmetric binary tree with branching angle \(\theta = 60^\circ\) (or \(45^\circ\)–\(90^\circ\) variants) and branch contraction factor \(r\) achieves exact self-contact of its limiting canopy when the geometric series \(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty} r^k = \frac{r}{1-r}\) matches the unit trunk, i.e. \(r^2 + r - 1 = 0 \implies r = \frac{1}{\phi} = \phi - 1\).

  3. POS-7.02 · Non-Overlapping Sierpiński-Pentagon (:math:`r = 1/phi^2`): Replacing a regular pentagon with five corner-touching scaled copies of ratio \(r\) without overlap requires \(1 - r = r\phi\)? More precisely, along the base of length \(1\), two scaled pentagons of base \(r\) plus the central gap of width \(r/\phi\)? In fact, two adjacent scaled pentagons touch at a vertex when \(r + r\phi = 1 \implies r(1 + \phi) = 1 \implies r = \frac{1}{\phi^2} = \frac{3 - \sqrt{5}}{2}\). Its Hausdorff similarity dimension is:

    \[D_H = \frac{\ln 5}{\ln(\phi^2)} = \frac{\ln 5}{2 \ln \phi} \approx 1.67228.\]
