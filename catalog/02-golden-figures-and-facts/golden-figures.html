Golden Geometric Figures (Ch. 4)¶

Source Locus: Chapter 4 (pp. 81–190 · Proofs pp. 312–325) · Cross-Links: Euclid IV.10–11, geometor/polyhedra

  1. Whirling-Square Golden Rectangles: A rectangle of aspect ratio \(\phi : 1\) is self-similar under gnomonic dissection: removing a \(1 \times 1\) square leaves a \(1 \times (\phi - 1) = 1 \times \frac{1}{\phi}\) golden rectangle. Iterating this dissection produces a nested sequence of squares whose quarter-circle arcs approximate the logarithmic golden spiral \(r(\theta) = r_0 \phi^{2\theta/\pi}\).

  2. POS-4.01 · Semicircle on Square Side Cutting Midpoint-Diagonal in Golden Ratio:

    In square ABCD of side \(2\) with base midpoint M \(=(1,0)\), the semicircle cM of radius \(1\) cuts the midpoint-diagonal DM (length \(\sqrt{5}\)) at P and extends to E. Since \(|DP| = \sqrt{5} - 1 = 2/\phi\) and diameter \(|PE| = 2\), the ratio PE / DP equals \(\phi\) (build_pos_4_01()).

  3. Golden Triangles & Golden Gnomons (Euclid IV.10): - Acute Golden Triangle (:math:`72^circtext{–}72^circtext{–}36^circ`): Leg-to-base ratio \(\phi : 1\). Bisecting a base angle partitions it into a smaller \(72^\circ\text{–}72^\circ\text{–}36^\circ\) triangle and an obtuse golden gnomon. - Obtuse Golden Gnomon (:math:`36^circtext{–}36^circtext{–}108^circ`): Base-to-leg ratio \(\phi : 1\). Every regular pentagon and pentagram decomposes exclusively into these two triangles.

  4. POS-4.02 · Folded Paper-Strip Overhand Knot Regular Pentagon: Tying a flat overhand knot in a uniform-width paper strip of width \(w\) and creasing it flat produces a regular pentagon of side \(s = w / \sin 72^\circ = \frac{4w}{\sqrt{10 + 2\sqrt{5}}}\) and diagonal-to-side ratio \(\phi\).

  5. The Golden Ellipse: An ellipse with semi-major axis \(a\) and semi-minor axis \(b\) satisfying \(a / b = \phi\) has eccentricity \(e = \sqrt{1 - 1/\phi^2} = 1/\sqrt{\phi}\) and focal-to-minor ratio \(c/b = 1\) (since \(c^2 = a^2 - b^2 = b^2(\phi^2 - 1) = b^2\phi\)? Wait: \(\phi^2 - 1 = \phi\), so \(c/b = \sqrt{\phi}\) and \(a/c = \sqrt{\phi}\)!).

  6. POS-4.03 · The Golden Cuboid (:math:`1 times phi times phi^2`) & Platonic Solids: A rectangular box with edge lengths \(1, \phi, \phi^2\) has space diagonal \(d = \sqrt{1 + \phi^2 + \phi^4} = \sqrt{4\phi^2} = 2\phi\) (verify_golden_cuboid()), volume \(V = \phi^3\), and surface area \(S = 2(\phi + \phi^2 + \phi^3) = 4\phi^3\). In 3D, three mutually orthogonal golden rectangles form the 12 vertices of a regular icosahedron, \((\pm 1, \pm \phi, 0)\) and cyclic permutations.
