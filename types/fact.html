Kepler’s Golden Right Triangle (Ch. 2)

A right triangle ABC has side lengths in geometric progression \(1 : \sqrt{\phi} : \phi\) if and only if its hypotenuse AB is divided by the altitude from C in the golden ratio, and its legs satisfy \(1^2 + (\sqrt{\phi})^2 = \phi^2\).

Numerical Value & Algebraic Properties (Ch. 3)

The golden ratio \(\phi = \frac{1 + \sqrt{5}}{2}\) is the unique positive root of \(x^2 - x - 1 = 0\). Every integer power \(\phi^n\) linearizes over \(\mathbb{Q}(\phi)\) via Fibonacci coefficients: \(\phi^n = F_n \phi + F_{n-1}\).
